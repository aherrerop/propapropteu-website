# propapropteu.com — competitor-eye review (2026-11-14)

Written as if a competitor were looking at the site. Compared with:
- **Spanish film-supply shops:** Cinetools (Madrid/Barcelona/Tenerife), plus prop-rental houses listed by the Barcelona-Catalunya Film Commission and the Madrid Film Office.
- **A large US film-gear shop:** Filmtools.
- **Custom prop / 3D-fabrication studios:** Geppetto Studios, H3DP, WhiteClouds, IPFL, There You Have It 3D.

---

## 1. What a competitor would think in 30 seconds

> "Nice look, clearly people who know sets. But there's one real product, two test products at 5 €, grey placeholders where the photos should be, no proof they've worked on anything, and you can only pay by bank transfer. Not a threat yet. The real threat is the *idea*: original products designed by set dressers, plus 'we make what doesn't exist' — nobody in Spain sells that online."

The positioning is strong. The site doesn't prove it yet.

---

## 2. Strengths (keep them)

1. **A position nobody else holds online.** Spanish shops either *rent* props (Artigau, Mateos, Enseres) or *resell* other brands' expendables (Cinetools). Original products designed by working set dressers, plus custom fabrication, is unique.
2. **Credibility from the inside.** "Tested on set", a Senior Set Dresser on the team, datasheets with real loads (8 kg) — that's the language buyers in an art department trust.
3. **A clean, editorial look.** Bebas Neue + Roboto, lots of white space, one orange accent. It looks more premium than the Spanish competitors, which look like generic shop templates.
4. **Three languages** (CA/ES/EN), which matters for Barcelona's international shoots. Cinetools only has two.
5. **A proper Request-a-prop flow** (files, deadline, delivery, budget). The studios compared mostly have a plain contact form, and Geppetto has no form at all.
6. **Legal and after-sales are in order:** withdrawal, 3-year guarantee, intellectual property, invoices sent automatically. Many small shops skip this.
7. **The back office is solid:** orders go straight into the to-do list, with automatic invoices and two customer emails. That lets two people run it.

---

## 3. Weak points, most serious first

### Critical (hurts credibility today)
1. **Test products are public.** "Prueba" and "Prueba 2" at 5 € are in the shop. That is the first thing a competitor or buyer would notice.
2. **One real product.** A shop with one item reads as "not open yet." The home page is just a single ATLAS slide; there's nothing to scroll.
3. **Placeholders everywhere:** About photo, team portraits, the six "our work" squares, all six packs, and the [bracketed] bios.
4. **No proof:** no productions, credits, client names, testimonials, or video of a product working. Every studio compared shows a gallery or names clients; Cinetools lists the associations it sponsors and its clients.
5. **Pages contradict each other:**
   - *Shipping & returns* says "we prepare your order in 1–3 days" and "track it from your account" (customer accounts don't exist).
   - FAQ and terms say "made to order, up to 30 days".
   - Product pages say "In stock" for made-to-order items.
   - The cookie banner mentions "the payment provider's cookies"; there's no card payment yet.
   - The Packs page promises "accounts and recurring billing", which doesn't exist.

### High (loses sales)
6. **Bank transfer only.** It's slow for a crew that needs something tomorrow. Cinetools takes card, Bizum and PayGold; Filmtools has business accounts and financing.
7. **No sense of speed.** Shoots run on urgency. Cinetools sells "2-hour delivery in Madrid, Barcelona and Tenerife". You are *in* Barcelona and say nothing about same-day pickup or courier.
8. **Prices only include VAT.** Production companies think in prices before VAT (Cinetools shows prices without VAT), and there's no volume price ("10 × ATLAS").
9. **Links shared on WhatsApp look bad.** Crews share links on WhatsApp. The preview image is an SVG logo, which WhatsApp, Facebook and LinkedIn don't show, and product links have no image or title of their own.
10. **Hard to find on Google.**
   - All three languages share one address, so Google sees one language.
   - Page titles are fixed in Catalan even when the page shows Spanish ("Attrezzo, eines i materials" over a Spanish page).
   - Product pages aren't in the sitemap.
   - There's no hreflang, no Google Business Profile, and no listing in the film-commission directories where competitors appear.
11. **No measurement.** No analytics, so you can't tell what people look at or where they drop out.

### Medium (polish)
12. **Mixed languages and spelling:** "About" stays English in ES/CA while the other tabs are translated; "Attrezzo" (Catalan/Italian) next to "atrezo" (Spanish); some fixed texts (skip link, payment banners, "Cistella") always appear in one language.
13. **Sort tabs that do nothing yet.** Relevance / A-Z / Z-A over 1–3 products adds noise.
14. **The product page doesn't sell the product in use:** no video or GIF, no "how it works" in 3 steps, no downloadable instructions, no "what's in the box".
15. **Request a prop gives no idea of cost or time.** There are no example prices, typical lead times, a rush option, or a confidentiality promise. Productions are secretive, so an NDA line matters.
16. **Three accent colours** on the numbered steps (orange/purple/teal) while the brand uses only orange elsewhere. The WhatsApp bubble covers content on phones.
17. **Accessibility:** most images have empty alt text, and the "skip to content" link is only in Spanish.

## 5. Recommendations by area

### A. Fix before promoting the site (quick, no new features)
- Unpublish *Prueba* / *Prueba 2* (or use a "hidden" product state).
- Make delivery promises match everywhere: one line such as "Made to order in Barcelona · usually ready in X days · pickup or courier." Use it on product pages, Shipping, FAQ and Terms, and replace "In stock" with "Made to order".
- Fix the Shipping page: remove "from your account" and the 1–3-day promise, or make them true.
- Cookie banner: either remove it (the site only stores language and cart, which needs no consent) or make its text match the cookie policy.
- Packs: hide the page and footer link until you decide; it currently promises things that don't exist.
- Use a PNG/JPG share image (1200×630) for the site, and the main photo for each product.

### B. Home
Turn the single slide into a short scroll:
1. Hero (keep).
2. "What we do" in three blocks: Shop · Request a prop · For productions.
3. Featured products (3–4 cards).
4. ATLAS in action (10-second loop video).
5. Credits strip: "Our work has been on: …" (titles or logos, with permission).
6. Request-a-prop band.
7. Barcelona / urgent line with WhatsApp.

The `home.*` texts for some of these already exist in the language files.

### C. Shop
- Grow the catalogue: even 6–8 items (variants, kits such as "ATLAS × 10", consumables you already print) change the impression.
- Show the price without VAT under the price with VAT; add volume price steps.
- Hide the sort tabs until there are 8+ products; keep the tag chips.
- Add a "Can't find it? Request a prop →" card at the end of the grid.

### D. Product page
- Video/GIF first, then photos. Add a "How it works" 1-2-3 section.
- Show "Made to order · ready in X days" and the delivery options next to the price.
- Downloadable PDF datasheet / instructions; "What's in the box".
- Volume prices; "Need 50 for a production? → quote".
- A per-product share image (needs a small build step or a per-product static page).

### E. Request a prop
- Example jobs with rough price and lead time ("Replica phone, 2 days, from 40 €").
- An **Urgent** checkbox (< 72 h) and a "confidential / NDA" line.
- Expected reply time ("we answer within one working day").

### F. About
- Real photos of the workshop and the two of you (the list is in `PICTURES-TO-REPLACE.txt`).
- Credits: productions each of you worked on, with IMDb links. This is the most persuasive thing you can add.
- Bios: one line on craft, one human or funny line.

### G. New pages or tabs to consider
- **Our work / Productions:** gallery + credits (could merge the "our work" section of Request a prop).
- **For productions (B2B):** pay on invoice, prices without VAT, volume prices, rush jobs, recurring supply (where Packs would live if you keep them).
- **Guides (later, for Google):** short how-tos such as "Hanging frames on set flats without damage". Each one ends at a product.

### H. Trust and conversion
- Card + Bizum (Stripe) as soon as possible; keep transfer for companies.
- Put the WhatsApp line in the header or product page for urgent jobs.
- Collect 3–5 testimonials from set dressers or art directors you know.

### I. Search, sharing and measurement
- One address per language (`/es/`, `/ca/`, `/en/`) with hreflang, and page titles/descriptions in each language.
- Product pages in the sitemap.
- Google Business Profile (workshop in Barcelona).
- Film-commission and Crew United listings.
- Cookieless analytics (e.g., Plausible or GoatCounter): no banner needed.

### J. Style consistency
- One accent colour (orange) for the numbered steps; purple/teal only if they become a deliberate brand system.
- One spelling decision per language ("atrezo" ES / "attrezzo" CA) and translate "About" or deliberately keep every tab in English.
- Same photo style everywhere: grey #dcdbd8 background for products, real workshop shots for people.
- Move the WhatsApp bubble up on phones so it doesn't cover the cart or forms.

---

## 6. Suggested order

1. **This week (credibility):** remove the test products, align the delivery texts, hide Packs, fix the share image, add real photos and bios.
2. **Next (sales):** Stripe card + Bizum, "made to order / Barcelona pickup" line, prices without VAT and volume prices, urgent option on Request a prop.
3. **Then (proof):** credits + Our work page, ATLAS video, testimonials, home page sections.
4. **Then (being found):** per-language addresses, sitemap with products, Google Business Profile, directory listings, analytics.
5. **Later:** B2B page / pay on invoice, newsletter, guides.
